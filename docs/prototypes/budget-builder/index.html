Where The Data Comes From

Every number in this tool is an estimate meant for learning, not a quote. Here's what sits behind each one so you can check it yourself.

Job Pay
Median annual wages for 20 occupations, each labeled with its federal SOC code (for example, Registered Nurse is 29-1141). Figures follow the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics program, which publishes national and state wages by occupation every May. bls.gov/oes Median means half the people in that job earn more and half earn less.
Pay By Location
Your state or ZIP applies a single cost-of-living multiplier to the national median, ranging from about 0.85 in the lowest-cost states to about 1.20 in Washington, D.C. Real BLS state and metro wage tables are more precise than one multiplier, and a live version of this tool should read them directly. State wage tables
Housing, Food, Transportation
Starting amounts are drawn from typical U.S. household spending patterns published in the Bureau of Labor Statistics Consumer Expenditure Survey, then adjusted by the same state multiplier. The three slider stops are a low, average, and high version of each category, not survey percentiles. bls.gov/cex
The 30% Housing Guideline
The federal standard that housing costing more than 30% of income counts as a cost burden. It comes from U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development affordability rules and is why this tool flags housing above that share. HUD on affordability
Federal Income Tax
Calculated from your salary using the seven federal marginal brackets, 10% through 37%, after subtracting the standard deduction for a single filer. Only your income above each bracket threshold is taxed at that higher rate, which is why the total is lower than the top rate you land in. IRS brackets
Social Security And Medicare
Fixed federal payroll tax rates that every employee pays: 6.2% for Social Security, applied only up to the annual wage base limit, and 1.45% for Medicare with no cap. Your employer pays a matching amount you never see. SSA wage base · IRS payroll rates
State Income Tax
A flat estimated rate per state applied to your income. Nine states, Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming, tax no wage income, so this line shows $0 there. States with graduated brackets are simplified to one representative rate. State rate table
Student Loan Payments
Estimated monthly payments for a standard 10-year repayment plan across small, average, and large borrowing amounts. Actual payments depend on how much you borrow, your interest rate, and which repayment plan you choose. Federal loan simulator
Health Insurance
A typical monthly premium for a single adult buying coverage on their own. If a job provides insurance, your employer usually covers a large share, so your cost would be lower. KFF premium survey
Savings Targets
The 5%, 10%, and 20% stops reflect the common advice to save at least 10% of income, with 20% as a stretch goal. There is no single official rule here.
One Caution

These amounts are built into the tool, not pulled live from a government database, and tax law and wages change every year. Check the current BLS and IRS figures before you rely on a number for a real decision. Start here: BLS wages, IRS tax rates, CFPB money tools.
